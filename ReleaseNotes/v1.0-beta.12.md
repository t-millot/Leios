## Speed is a slider now

**Speed** used to be four fixed choices: macOS, Low, Medium and High. It is now a switch, **Use
macOS scroll speed**, and underneath it a slider from Low to High in steps of a tenth.

Low, Medium and High are exact points on the slider and scroll exactly as they did. Everything in
between is a blend of the same tuning, so a setting a little faster than Low is just that — not a
new curve to get used to. While the macOS switch is on, the slider and **Precise** step out of the
way, since both shape the curve macOS speed replaces.

An app profile can pin Speed like any other setting, and pins the switch and the slider together:
a profile set to Low stays at Low wherever the main slider goes.

If you use iCloud sync with a Mac still on an older beta, that Mac reads the nearest of Low, Medium
and High rather than rejecting your settings.

## Smoother at reading pace on High smoothness

Turn the wheel slowly, a few ticks a second, and scrolling came out in pulses: every tick jumped
straight to its own speed and coasted down until the next. On **High**, a tick that arrives while
the page is still moving now picks up from the speed it is already going and eases into its own, so
a steady hand gives a steady scroll. A tick from a standstill, and the other smoothness settings,
are unchanged.

## Turning the wheel back stops the scroll

Reversing the wheel against a scroll that is still gliding is meant to stop it on the first tick,
then follow the wheel from the next. Leios got that backwards: the first reversed tick scrolled a
little the other way and the second stopped it dead. It now stops on the first, which is what makes
a flick back a way to put on the brakes.

## Quick and Precise let go when you do

Since beta 8, holding the **Quick** or **Precise** scroll key could leave it in force after you let
go, for every scroll in that direction until you reversed. Leios reads those keys when a scroll
starts, and a scroll starts after a pause — which Leios was measuring from a real mouse as 42 times
shorter than it was, so an eight-second pause read as a fifth of a second.

The same mismeasurement fed the acceleration, so fast scrolling now ramps up on how quickly your
wheel is really turning. If a fast flick travels differently from beta 11, that is why.

## Full frame rate on the display you are scrolling

With two displays at different refresh rates, a scroll could get stuck on the slower one's clock:
start scrolling on a 120 Hz screen while a glide from a 60 Hz one was still running, and it drew at
60 Hz, and kept doing so until the pointer moved a fair way. Every scroll now runs at the refresh
rate of the display it is on, and switches mid-glide without a stutter.

Separately, unplugging a display in the middle of a scroll could leave smooth scrolling dead until
something reset it. It recovers on the next scroll now.

## A tidier Statistics screen

The five totals sit on one row, every chart title lives inside its chart, and charts of categories
all read the same way: names down the side, counts at the end of each bar. Two Macs with the same
name no longer merge into one bar.

## Nothing to redo

Your settings, per-app profiles, button mappings and Accessibility permission all carry across an
update. Leios can install this one itself if you are on beta 11 with **Include pre-release
versions** turned on.
